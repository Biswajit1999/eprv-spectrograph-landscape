Precision RV Spectrograph Literature Atlas

A paper-by-paper account of optical and near-infrared Doppler instruments: how each system is configured, what its authors tested, what the measurements establish, what remains difficult, and which next checks may be useful.

Scope and authorship

An Independent, Source-Linked Synthesis

This guide grew from Biswajit Jana's literature review while working on the EXOhSPEC testbed. It summarizes published instrument designs, calibration strategies, performance measurements and unresolved challenges; it does not present an independent re-reduction of every instrument's data.

The work has not been formally validated by every principal investigator or instrument team. Primary papers and dated observatory sources are linked so that statements can be checked, challenged and revised.

Calibration and extraction

Three common measurement architectures

From extracted spectrum to velocity

The pipeline is part of the measurement

A stable optical bench is necessary, but the reported radial velocity also depends on how pixels, wavelengths, tellurics, templates and timestamps are modelled.

01

Cross-correlation function

A weighted line mask compresses thousands of spectral lines into an average profile. It is fast and interpretable, but the mask, rejected regions and profile fit define the measurement.

Baranne et al. (1996)
02

Template matching and forward models

Least-squares methods compare each observation with a high-signal empirical template; broader forward models can also fit the wavelength solution, line-spread function, calibration and contamination. More retained information also means more assumptions to validate.

Zechmeister et al. (2018)
03

Reference frame and exposure time

Earth's orbital and rotational motion is removed with a relativistic barycentric correction. At EPRV scales, the correction must follow the photon-weighted exposure—often as a function of wavelength—not just the shutter midpoint.

Wright & Eastman (2014) · Tronsgaard et al. (2019)
04

Detector and atmosphere

Pixel stitching, charge transfer, persistence, non-linearity and micro-tellurics can move or reshape lines. Calibration cannot automatically remove effects that do not share the stellar light path or the same detector response.

Dumusque et al. (2015)
Why solar feeds matter

The nearest star becomes a high-cadence systems test

Small solar telescopes feed disk-integrated sunlight through the same spectrograph and reduction chain used at night. The dense cadence and external solar context help expose instrumental, calibration and stellar line-shape effects. A solar RMS still mixes weather, observing strategy, pipeline choices and solar variability, so it is not a universal instrument floor or a promise for other stars.
